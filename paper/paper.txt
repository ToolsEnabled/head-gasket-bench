                                                     Abstract

         Can an AI agent carry out a mechanical repair, rather than only describe one? We present

a head-gasket replacement benchmark built on a detailed CAD model of a 1990s 1.6-litre SOHC

inline-four engine. Working through tool calls, the agent must look up the service specifications,

dismantle the engine in an order the geometry allows, measure the head, block and bolts, choose

the correct parts, tighten the head bolts in the specified stages and sequence, and reassemble the

engine. We adopt TheAgentCompany’s full and partial completion formulas without

modification; our checkpoints are binary and checked by deterministic code. As a domain-

specific cost, every action is also charged an estimated technician time, so mistakes and repeated

work carry a labour cost. In exploratory runs, one per model on the standard job, no agent

completed the repair fully correctly. GPT-6-Astra reached the highest partial score, 41.7% (15 of

18 checkpoint points), with all ten head bolts tightened correctly and 1,404 seconds of penalty

time. GPT-6-Luna scored 36.1%. GPT-6.1-Sol scored 8.3% and left the engine disassembled.

The most common failures were repeated work, repeating requests the environment had refused,

and finishing with parts not refitted. These are single runs; they describe behaviour and do not

rank the models.

Model names are as requested from the provider at xhigh reasoning effort; the serving system did not attest the
model identity.

                                                 1 Introduction

         A correct repair depends on decisions made along the way. A bolt tightened to the final

value can still have an incorrect stage history. A head cannot be removed through an obstruction

merely because its final pose is legal. A plausible written procedure does not show that an agent

can maintain these constraints while acting.

         We study a head-gasket replacement on a real 1990s 1.6 L SOHC inline-four. The agent
works through tools against a detailed CAD-based engine model on a prepared service stand. The

surrounding vehicle is outside the task. Specifications must be found in the service-information

corpus rather than supplied in the task prompt. This benchmark follows Engine Bench

(https://toolsenabled.ai/bench/, code at https://github.com/ToolsEnabled/engine-bench) and uses

the same engine model: Engine Bench asks an agent to build the engine, and this benchmark asks

it to repair one.

        We follow TheAgentCompany’s organization and aggregate scoring [1]. Our contribution

is the repair application: an environment where part identities, access, measurements, tightening

stages and assembly completeness matter jointly. We do not propose a new scoring formula or

statistical method. Technician time adds a domain-specific cost alongside computational cost.

        The report distinguishes the task specification from supported software behavior and

physical qualifications. Current results describe exploratory standard-job trajectories; the larger

comparative collection remains pending. Historical scripted references and model attempts retain

their actual rules and evaluator versions.


                    2 Benchmark desiderata and comparison to other benchmarks

        The benchmark should require consequential actions, preserve intermediate state, and

provide an inspectable basis for every score. An agent should be able to discover the information

needed to act, and the evaluator should distinguish an incorrect repair from an unavailable

measurement or an apparatus failure.

        TheAgentCompany evaluates professional work through workplace tools and simulated

colleague conversations, combining full completion with intermediate checkpoints. We adapt its

reporting structure to a component-level repair procedure. The contribution is this application

and its task specification, rather than a new evaluation method [1].
       ScienceWorld links scientific knowledge to actions in a text simulation. SimulCost

studies accuracy and computation cost in solver tuning. CompMat-Bench grades materials-

research inputs and outputs, while its longer workflows also execute calculations. ReFract tests

action trajectories against role-specific boundaries. DeepTest tests automotive information

retrieval for omitted warnings. These settings illuminate different aspects of procedural work;

their scores measure different targets and are not directly comparable [2] [11] [12] [13] [14].

       Evaluation practice also shapes the design. AI Agents That Matter motivates cost-

controlled comparisons, suitable holdouts and reproducible evaluation. The Agentic Benchmark

Checklist distinguishes task validity from outcome validity. Harness-disclosure work makes the

execution configuration part of the experimental condition, while the double-measurement study

shows why evaluator truth and scaffold ownership require separate scrutiny. These sources

motivate explicit task predicates, isolation, configuration disclosure and accounting for

unavailable observations [3] [4] [5] [6].

       Our desiderata are a documented service procedure, separate component identities,

geometry-based access, stateful tool actions, task-specific deterministic checks, and reproducible

initial conditions. Difficulty should arise from the job rather than hidden specifications or

arbitrary tool failures. Missing service limits and unresolved access must remain visible.


                                            3 Environment

       The game presents a prepared engine, tools, replacement supplies and service

information. The interface supports looking, inspection, measurement, service search, removal,

installation, cleaning, replacement, tightening, timing checks and final submission. Every action

refers to a specific target; attempts and state changes are recorded separately.

       CAD bodies retain individual identities. A service-body index links procedure names to
geometry, but a display or index entry alone does not prove that a part can be withdrawn.

Geometry and access checks must determine permitted removal order. Unknown constraints

cannot be interpreted as permission.

       Clamp mechanics provide state changes and observations such as bolt stretch, gasket

condition and local clamp response. Unloaded service flatness is supplied or measured state;

loaded head bow and bolt stretch are model responses. Available models include idealized

material and contact behavior. These models support environment realism within their stated

scope; they are not a full engine simulation or a validation of service life.

       Neither a soak nor an engine run is part of scoring. The earlier operating-soak approach

was dropped because the intended outcome is completion of the repair procedure against a

correct reference. This is a design decision, not an engine-durability result.


                                           4 Task structure

       Each task comprises an instruction, a fixed starting state, a service corpus, weighted

checkpoints and a deterministic evaluator. The proposed evaluator will read the final state and

chronological action record. Binary checkpoint decisions will expose the supporting observations

and the reason for each failed check.

       The agent is asked to complete the repair, not to reproduce an answer string. The correct

scripted baseline supplies the reference procedure and must satisfy every checkpoint. It defines

100% reference accuracy; that requirement is distinct from the complete original-rule references

reported separately and from validation under the amended rules.


                                        4.1 Evaluation metrics

       Following TheAgentCompany, full completion is S_full = 1 if every checkpoint passes,

and 0 otherwise. Partial completion is S_partial = 0.5 × (points earned / total points) + 0.5 ×
S_full [1].

        The aggregate formula is used unchanged. This application uses binary deterministic

checkpoints. The template also permits partial credit within checkpoints and LLM evaluation for

some tasks; those mechanisms are not used for the proposed repair checks [1].

        Success is mean S_full and Score is mean S_partial. Steps counts model calls, not tool

calls. API-equivalent Cost is prompt tokens multiplied by the prompt-token rate plus completion

tokens multiplied by the completion-token rate, under the template’s no-caching convention.

Token counts, rate date, model identity and currency must be reported. A subscription fee is not

an API price; unavailable rates or model-call counts remain unavailable [1].

        Technician time is the sum of cited action charges and mistake-correction charges,

reported as productive and penalty components beside the scripted baseline. It is charged time,

not a measured mechanic’s elapsed time or a simulated delay. Mistakes by type, agent wall time

and tool calls are additional measures.

        Secondary measures include each of the ten head bolts’ condition, both torque stages and

sequence position, plus checkpoint pass rates and failure categories. The explicit head-bolt group

defines the denominator; covers and other fasteners do not enter it.


                                             4.2 Workflow

        Initialization restores the declared starting state, selects the variant, verifies the

environment and evaluator versions, and starts the isolated collector. The task prompt identifies

the job; the service corpus supplies specifications. Execution records each tool request, response,

state transition and charge. Finalization evaluates the repair and preserves the complete report.

        An end report is required after normal submission, budget exhaustion, early stopping,

interruption or an apparatus refusal. It retains observed fields and named unknowns. Calling
finish does not by itself establish complete or correct work. Earlier mistakes remain in the action

record; the checkpoint specification must state how later corrections affect final accuracy and

charged corrective work.

                                         5 Task creation

       The draft task set varies the condition of the same repair problem rather than introducing
unrelated jobs. It includes a standard job, a stretched bolt, a warped head, an incorrect gasket

among available parts, and a worn timing belt. Each variant needs a realizable starting state,

appropriate observations, a correct baseline and a tested evaluator before use.

       Checkpoint curation follows the service procedure. It covers lookup, disassembly, timing

preparation, loosening, cleaning, measurement, part choice, tightening and reassembly. Point

values express task importance. They must be reviewed and frozen before collecting comparative

runs; they must not be tuned to a model ranking.

       The historical per-operation labor table consists of analyst estimates and charges

successful actions only. Later scripted reports include corrective penalties, also as analyst

estimates. Neither establishes the cited professional action-time and correction schedule required

by the new design. Labor sources, charge units and rules for blocked attempts and rework remain

to be established.

       The earlier snapshot did not demonstrate the full task set. Later original-rule records

contain complete references for every configured primary variant, including a software head-

machining route and a separate head-replacement alternative. These records do not establish

physical machining, exact service applicability or completion under the amended rules. Selected

component transitions must remain bound to their actual runtime and configuration.

       Each task should specify the starting component condition, the information visible to the

agent, permitted service actions and the state or history evidence needed for every checkpoint.

For each proposed variant, a reference procedure should demonstrate that the task is feasible

under the same tool contract. Review should cover correct completion, plausible incorrect

completion and unavailable measurements before model collection. The current task variants are

design requirements; legacy fixture names do not establish their implementation.
                                        6 Baseline agents

       The planned agent is Codex CLI with only the environment’s MCP tools, run through an

isolated collector. The planned model arms are GPT-6.1-Sol, GPT-6-Astra and GPT-6-Luna. A

Claude arm is a possible later extension.

       The scripted correct repair is the environment reference, not a competing language

model. It must execute the intended job with valid access and actions and satisfy the complete

checkpoint set. Deliberately incorrect jobs test evaluator sensitivity to wrong order, wrong

torque, omitted stages, incorrect parts, missed measurements and timing errors.

       The exploratory run budget and earlier attempts remain in the accounting. Actual

standard-task observations are reported below; the larger model-by-variant matrix remains

pending. Requested and served model identifiers, transport refusals and stopping states retain

their recorded meanings.

       Report requested and returned model identifiers separately, with agent version, prompt,

decoding controls, budgets, tools, context handling and retry policy. A configured model name

does not prove the served model. Tool transport or process-cleanup fixtures do not establish

production collection behavior [5].

       An agent condition consists of the model and its execution harness. The collection record

should specify the declared model version, inference settings, instructions, context policy, tools,

stopping rule and retries. Conditions should receive the same task information and evaluation

contract. Complete original-rule references are separate from the earlier incomplete diagnostics.

References under the amended rules still need their own validation. Scripted usage fields and

unavailable model telemetry must retain their recorded meanings [5].

7.1 Common failures

(a) Repeated and excess work
       Repeated work accounted for 213 operations and 10,350 penalty seconds for GPT-6.1-

Sol, 15 operations and 630 seconds for GPT-6-Astra, and 191 operations and 9,840 seconds for

GPT-6-Luna. Sol and Astra each loosened the ten head bolts in four passes instead of three. Their

extra passes—Sol 220–229 and Astra 240–249—each added 420 seconds and lost CP04's one

point. Luna removed the head and fitted the gasket again at 692–693; both operations were

charged as repeated work.


(b) Repeating refused requests without changing the repair
       GPT-6-Luna made 40 consecutive restoration requests at 424–463, receiving the same

refusal every time: "Restore attachments after all selected service positions are installed". The

requests targeted different fasteners, but no parts were refitted between them to meet that

requirement. This block cost 1,200 seconds. All 43 restoration refusals were included in Luna's

110 invalid actions, charged 4,890 seconds overall. The refusal named the unmet requirement but

not the missing parts; the next environment version adds the requested item and the number of

parts not yet refitted.


(c) Parts and consumables
       GPT-6.1-Sol discarded the old gasket at 276, then fitted its only new gasket at 327 by

installing it directly from stock rather than through the replace action, which records a new part

as the replacement for the original. Later replace requests at 438 and 661 both returned

"Replacement supplies unavailable". The procedure does not refit a multi-layer steel gasket once

removed, so removing it at 660 left Sol with no usable gasket.


(d) Order-of-work requirements
       GPT-6-Astra released a timing-cover screw at 112, then tried to remove it at 113 while

the crank pulley was still in place, blocking the screw's withdrawal route. The refusal cost 30
seconds and CP02's two points. Sol's three blocked removals and Luna's seven similarly cost 90

and 210 seconds, with CP02 unmet in both runs. Separately, Luna removed the head at 692 and

placed the final gasket at 693 without cleaning in between. Earlier cleaning at 355–358 did not

meet this requirement; CP05 lost one point.


(e) Finishing with work outstanding
        GPT-6-Luna submitted finish at 807 with 100 fastener and connector restorations still

outstanding. It failed the two-point final-reassembly check, CP12, and received a further 30-

second missing-restoration charge. GPT-6.1-Sol never submitted finish: after 659–660, the head,

gasket and all ten head bolts remained in the tray. Its final state failed the five two-point

completion checks, CP08–CP12, despite earlier successful tightening at 339–358.

       The final scores were 3/18 for GPT-6.1-Sol, 15/18 for GPT-6-Astra and 13/18 for GPT-6-

Luna. Penalty totals were 12,012, 1,404 and 14,970 seconds respectively, agreed by the current

version of both scorers. The earlier independent scorer recorded 11,952 penalty seconds for Sol

(Appendix F). These examples show how repeated work, unmet prerequisites and incomplete

reassembly accumulated costs even when individual repair operations succeeded.




                               8 Implications and future directions

       A repair environment can test whether an agent uses information while maintaining a

changing physical-state model. The application extends a professional-task benchmark pattern

into mechanical procedure execution through software tools. Its value depends on accurate task

constraints and evaluators, not on adding a new aggregate score. Engine Bench and this

benchmark share one engine model and together cover building the engine and repairing it; both

are published at https://toolsenabled.ai/bench/, with code at
https://github.com/ToolsEnabled/engine-bench.

       Future work should complete the service-to-geometry mapping, validate references under

the amended rules, validate the technician-time estimates against published labour-time guides

and extend the original-rule software checks to the selected collection runtime. Collected

trajectories can then support analysis of procedural decisions and resource use. Broader tasks and

physical execution require separate evidence.

       The benchmark does not certify that an agent or robot can safely repair an engine. It

omits the surrounding vehicle, workshop dexterity and many material uncertainties.

ToolsEnabled, Inc. develops the environment and authors this report; this relationship should be

considered when interpreting the design. The exploratory attempts do not establish comparative

population performance.

Appendix B. Mechanical assumptions

The mechanical model links bolt loading, gasket compression and head response. The historical
rigid model and later elastic plate approximation have different scopes. A linear elastic
mechanical plate with a yield exclusion must not be described as a model of permanent head
deformation.
Separate generic material-law work carries gasket recovery, bolt stretch and sectional material
history. Its parameters and applicability are not selected-part validation. Finite order comparisons
show that an action history can affect transient component response; they do not establish lasting
damage, reliable repair classification or engine durability.
Material proxies, contact reduction and finite comparison limits remain explicit. Mechanical
state is used to make inspection and measurement meaningful. Checkpoint correctness is
determined by the reference procedure and recorded work, without operating the engine.

Appendix D. Agent details and harness disclosure

The collector must restrict the agent to the environment MCP interface and record the full
prompt, tool schema, requested model, returned model and execution configuration. Session state
and credentials must be isolated from unrelated work. The public report does not disclose
account identifiers.
The recorded historical collector includes wrapper and process-recovery evidence. A successful
owned-process cleanup test does not prove current model identity, account isolation or
production lifecycle behavior. These require tests against the exact collector used for study runs.
Record model calls, tool attempts, successful tool actions, prompt and completion tokens, cache
accounting if available, wall-clock start and end, retry events and termination reason. If a field
cannot be obtained, report it as unavailable and explain the measurement boundary. Do not infer
model calls from tool calls or price from subscription access.
Keep configuration and prompt versions alongside every attempt. Retries must retain their prior
records. A failure to execute the apparatus is distinct from an agent choosing an incorrect action.
Neither a cleanup failure nor a partial response should erase the evidence already collected.
The agent record should distinguish actions, observations and middleware behavior. Document
any automatic retries, preprocessing, state recovery or submission logic, so readers can tell which
decisions the model made. Store model-call counts separately from tool-call counts, and preserve
prompt and completion tokens, dated prices and elapsed time when those measurements are
available. Missing telemetry must remain unavailable rather than being inferred from action
counts [5] [6].
GPT-6-Luna's finish-action response exceeded the collector's message size limit and was not
captured; the game state at its finish action is reported.
Appendix E. Evaluation protocol

This condensed protocol governs the proposed comparison. The complete historical form and a
field-by-field amendment mapping accompany the report. The present design replaces earlier
survival endpoints, factorial settings and judge-based scoring; historical records are retained
without making them current requirements.
      Study design and approval The dated study design governs the repair benchmark. Record
      the approved questions, collection scope and amendments before examining outcomes.
      Collection beyond the exploratory allowance requires the owner's approval. Preserve
      excluded and superseded records, record changes and document the authority for
      publication.
      Task definitions and versions Fix the task variants, checkpoint definitions, weights,
      prompts and evaluator versions before agent collection. Screen each task against the
      correct reference procedure and review any ambiguous interpretation. Retain earlier
      versions separately and record the reason for every later change.
      Collection environment and validation Describe the collection environment, permitted
      tools, launch method, configuration and isolation checks. Specify validation frequency,
      scope and method before collection. Complete the applicable preflight and retain
      validation evidence for each batch. Tool-interface tests, operating-system isolation and
      provider qualification are separate checks; earlier fixtures do not establish the selected
      collection environment.
      Agent settings and telemetry Record requested and returned model identities, collection
      surface, prompts, resource limits and supported decoding settings. Preserve commands,
      diagnostics and revisions. Steps counts model calls; tool calls and interface turns are
      separate measures. Unavailable identity or usage fields remain unknown.
      Execution order and resources State funding, execution order, concurrency and retry
      scheduling. Give each attempt a unique identity, prevent duplicate starts and record
      scheduling changes after errors. Historical defaults are not the new approved collection
      plan. Report subscription use separately from token-based API-equivalent cost.
      Variants and sample allocation Specify the model-by-variant allocation, exploratory
      allowance, repeats and completion target before collection. The proposed variants remain
      draft until their definitions and implementations are validated. Retain charged attempts in
      the accounting, and obtain approval before expanding the full collection matrix.
Interrupted attempts and retries Retain every attempt, including interruptions, provider
failures and refusals. Classify model outcomes separately from apparatus failures. Fix
inclusion, retry and response-selection rules, deduplicate responses and reconcile batch
totals. Report observed checkpoint evidence and named unknowns for incomplete
attempts; do not silently remove them from the report or assign missing observations a
zero score.
Reference procedure and extraction Fix reference inputs and extraction rules, and use
unique run identifiers. Validate checkpoint decisions against the correct scripted procedure
and targeted defective jobs, with independently reviewed final state and action histories.
Record timeouts, completion evidence and evaluator revisions. The correct reference must
pass every applicable checkpoint; this requirement is separate from an observed validation
result.
Ambiguity and refusal Distinguish an agent refusal, a clarification request, an
unsupported task and a collection failure. State the classification rule and preserve the
original response. An unresolved apparatus specification is not automatically an agent
mistake. Omitting a required agent action under a valid fixed reference can fail its
checkpoint.
Scoring and uncertainty Use the template's full and partial completion measures. State
the Success denominator and Wilson interval, and the bootstrap sampling unit for Score
and technician time. Fix variant weighting, incomplete-attempt treatment and resampling
settings before collection. Preserve dependence among checkpoints and bolts within a run.
Earlier survival and factorial plans are superseded; exploratory checks do not establish
general model rankings.
Reporting and deviations Report counts through collection and analysis, separate
exploratory and later cohorts, and explain deviations. Show model calls, tool calls, tokens
and wall time separately. Report productive technician time, typed corrective penalties and
the correct reference time, preserving the distinction between cited durations and
estimates. Agent results remain pending collection; apparatus records are reported
separately.
Evaluator review This application uses deterministic checkpoint evaluators.
Independently review their decisions against final state and chronological records under a
stated manual-review plan. The earlier language-model judge threshold and independence
settings do not apply to this task. The manual-review sample and selection rule must be
specified rather than inferred from the historical form.

Appendix G. Limitations

One run per model; descriptive, not comparative. Technician-time charges are estimates
requiring validation against published labour-time guides. Game performance is not physical
repair validation.
The model observations are exploratory attempts on a standard configured task. Requested
model identifiers are known, but served identities and model-call Steps are unavailable.
Geometric visibility, software action checks and finite mechanical predictions establish different
aspects of the apparatus; they do not certify physical repair. Factory service applicability,
physical machining and fit, full service coverage, external contract identity and professional
labor-time sources remain qualified. Earlier incomplete diagnostics and complete original-rule
references stay separate from amended-rule model attempts. The larger comparative study
remains pending.
The benchmark covers a prepared engine and a narrow repair procedure. It omits vehicle-level
access, physical dexterity, workshop hazards and many inspection cues. Success in this game
cannot certify a physical repair or authorize deployment on real equipment.
Geometry is detailed but service access, component mapping and legal transitions are incomplete
in the reviewed snapshot. Complete original-rule software references are available for the
configured abnormal conditions; equivalent coverage under the amended rules remains to be
demonstrated. Service thresholds and orientation information must be resolved rather than
invented.
Mechanical models use generic material properties and finite comparisons. They support selected
state estimates but are not selected-part durability validation. No operating-engine endpoint
contributes to scoring.
Technician charges are a domain cost estimate rather than measured workshop performance. The
historical action table lacks the required professional time sources and correction schedule; later
scripted diagnostic charges remain analyst estimates. API-equivalent cost may be unavailable for
a returned model; that limitation must not be concealed by a subscription-price proxy.
A small set of related variants cannot establish broad repair competence or occupational
automation. Repeated runs, within-repair bolt observations and shared task structure create
dependence. The final analysis must state its population and sampling limits; exploratory checks
must not be presented as a confirmatory study [7] [8].
Data and code availability: the report is accompanied by a source index, numerical claim table,
evaluation specification, protocol amendment mapping and configuration records. Source
identifiers and complete content hashes are kept in these companions so that each substantive
claim can be inspected without turning the paper into a process log.
